# Which results are current, and which are superseded

Not everything in this directory is citable. The prototype results from the
semester-2 phase were produced **before** the five instrument defects were found
and fixed, so their numbers are not comparable with the numbers in the paper.
They are kept for provenance, not for citation.

## Superseded — do not quote in the paper

| Path | Why it cannot be cited |
|---|---|
| `h1_mvp_summary.md`, `h1_outputs.csv`, `h1_scored.csv` | 150-sample MVP. Old lexicon, English-only negation, truncated index |
| `h1_real_openi_mmcqsd/` | Same, plus the Open-i retrieval arm that top-1 hits 0.007 |
| `dataset_comparison/` | Four-dataset scoping study. Same instrument |

Three specific traps in those files:

1. **Hallucination is not an independent measure.** `hallucination` is exactly
   `1 - precision`, verified min = max = 1.0. `dataset_comparison` reports a
   "hallucination drop" of 0.993 for Open-i alongside a factual gain of 0.105;
   those are one result, restated, on an arm whose retrieval barely functioned.
2. **Negation was scored English-only**, so every Hinglish denial
   (`"rash nahi hai"`) counted as an assertion.
3. **The index encoded roughly 15% of each case**, against a 554-word median,
   while BM25 read the full document. That defect had reversed the main
   retrieval comparison.

`dataset_comparison` remains useful for exactly one thing: it is the evidence
behind choosing MMCQS over PubMedQA and MMedBench as the query source. Cite it
as a scoping decision, never as a measurement.
